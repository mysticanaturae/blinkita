01

THE ABDOMEN IS
MORE THAN THE ABDOMEN.

It is a space where body, breath, touch, sensation, history and the relationship you have with yourself meet.

At BLINKITA, Maya Abdominal Massage is not presented as yet another technique you have to add to the list of things you are supposed to do for yourself.

It is a way of learning a relationship with your body through conscious touch, structured self-massage and an understanding of the principles on which the practice rests.

And that is precisely why self-massage is central here.

I do not want you to know only how to receive the work.
I want you to know how to work with yourself as well.

04
RESPONSIBILITY TO THE BODY

WHEN YES.
WHEN NO.

We do not learn bodywork by blindly following a protocol. We also learn to recognize when a practice may be appropriate and when it is better to wait.

01

WHEN IT MAY BE APPROPRIATE

For people who want to better understand their body, develop a more conscious relationship with the abdominal and pelvic area, and learn a structured approach to self-touch.

Before beginning, we look at your current situation, your intention for the work and any circumstances that are relevant to practicing safely.

02

WHEN IT IS NOT RECOMMENDED

In cases of acute pain of unknown origin, fever or acute infection, recent injuries, after certain surgeries or procedures, in certain conditions during pregnancy, or in other circumstances where medical supervision is needed, the practice should be postponed or appropriate medical advice should be obtained before beginning.

If there is doubt,
we do not guess.
We check first.

Maya Abdominal Massage is a form of bodywork and education in self-touch. It does not replace medical diagnosis, treatment or urgent medical care.
05
KNOWLEDGE DOES NOT BEGIN WITH US.

IT IS PASSED ON.

Before Maya Abdominal Massage became a contemporary therapeutic practice, there was knowledge that was passed through people, hands, plants, the body, experience and generations.

Dr. Rosita Arvigo studied for thirteen years in Belize with Don Elijio Panti, a traditional Maya healer, and for ten years with Miss Hortence Robinson, an herbalist midwife and spiritual healer.

Her official biography describes this long apprenticeship as the foundation of her later work, which brought together traditional knowledge with her education and study of anatomy and physiology.

This is not the story of a single technique that appeared one day. pojavila.

It is a story of learning, observation, the passing on of knowledge and responsibility toward what you receive from a teacher.

My own path is part of this chain.

I studied Maya Abdominal Massage Therapy , with Dr. Rosita Arvigo, completed Level 3 Maya Spiritual Healing / Maya Medicine and also completed the teacher level of Maya Abdominal Massage.

That is why I do not simply practice this work today.

I TEACH IT.

I teach people how to correctly perform self-massage on themselves, and I lead 3-day foundational self-massage training based on the principles of Maya Abdominal Massage.

As part of my education, I also studied practices connected with vaginal steaming, which is included within the broader context of the Arvigo approach.

This is knowledge I respect.
A tradition I do not want to simplify.
And a practice I teach with responsibility.
