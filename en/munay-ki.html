MUNAY · KI

When love becomes a way of living.

Munay Ki is a contemporary system of initiations connected with shamanic and initiatory practices of the Andes and Amazon, and with teachings brought into contemporary practice through Alberto Villoldo, Marcela Lobos and their teachers. The name Munay Ki is commonly explained through Quechua as the power or energy of love. Here, love is not presented as a romantic idea, but as a quality of presence, openness of the heart, relationship with life and responsibility for what we create.

In this context, an initiation is an invitation into relationship. It is not a title, a reward or proof that someone has become more evolved. It is a transmission after which the real work begins — living what you have received, observing your patterns, changing your relationship with your body, with other people, with nature and with the world you participate in creating every day.

BLINKITA therefore does not approach Munay Ki as a collection of beautiful rituals that we complete and put away. It is approached as a path of remembering that continues long after the ceremony itself.

MY PATH

My Munay Ki journey began in 2008.

My work with Munay Ki did not begin yesterday. I received my first Munay Ki initiations in 2008. In that same year, I organized the first Munay Ki training in Slovenia, bringing teachers from abroad so that people here could experience these initiations directly.

In 2010 and 2011, in Switzerland, I also personally met Dr. Alberto Villoldo and Marcela Lobos during training in shamanic healing work. That period became an important part of my personal path and of the way I understand initiatory work, consciousness, the body and inner transformation today.

Enough time has passed that Munay Ki is no longer an idea I explain from the outside. It has become part of a path through which I learned to distinguish between ritual and actual practice, between beautiful words and a genuine change in the way we relate to life.

That is also the BLINKITA approach to Munay Ki: we are not looking for another identity to put on. We are looking for what has been there underneath fear, habits, old stories and learned ideas all along.

THE NINE RITES

Nine doors into one path.

The classical Munay Ki system consists of nine initiatory rites. Each has its own symbolism, quality and focus. At the same time, they are not best understood as nine separate destinations. Together they form a map of an inner path — moving through healing, protection, harmony, vision, relationship with time, Earth, future and creation.

The descriptions below use the symbolic language of the Munay Ki tradition. They are not medical diagnoses, and the initiations are not presented as substitutes for medical care. They belong to a ritual, symbolic and spiritual framework.

01

Healer Rite

A connection with a lineage of healing and with the possibility of transforming personal experience into wisdom, presence and compassion.

02

Bands of Power

Five symbolic energetic bands associated with Earth, Water, Fire, Air and light, connected with protection and groundedness.

03

Harmony Rite

Work with animal archetypes and inner balance, returning us toward greater integrity and a more conscious relationship with life.

04

Seer Rite

A symbolic awakening of inner vision — the capacity to perceive beyond the first visible layer and to see with greater clarity and compassion.

05

Daykeeper Rite

A connection with a lineage of wisdom and healing, and with inner balance, peace and the ability to move consciously through everyday life.

06

Wisdomkeeper Rite

A relationship with ancestral and future wisdom, learning, time and a wider perspective on one's own life.

07

Earthkeeper Rite

A deeper relationship with Earth, nature and responsibility for the world we participate in creating — not merely as an idea, but as a way of being.

08

Starkeeper Rite

A rite connected with the times to come, with a wider perspective on human evolution and with the possibility of consciously participating in our own transformation.

09

Creator Rite

An invitation to recognize that creation is not only a privilege but also a responsibility — what we create carries our intention, relationship and consequences.

HOMO LUMINOUS

From the human who survives to the human who remembers.

Within the language of Munay Ki, the expression Homo luminous appears as an image of a human being who begins to experience consciousness, body and life as having a luminous or subtle dimension. In some Munay Ki teachings, the term is connected with the idea of a future human being who moves beyond fear and separation toward wisdom, compassion and responsibility.

BLINKITA uses this language as a metaphysical and symbolic language. Homo luminous is not presented as a scientifically established new biological species of human. Likewise, claims about changing DNA or rapidly physically rejuvenating the human body are not presented here as scientifically proven facts.

Yet the question underneath the language is very real: What changes in a human being when we stop living only from old patterns and begin consciously choosing how we want to live?

This is where Munay Ki meets BLINKITA. Not in the promise of a miraculous body, but in the exploration of our relationship with the body, time, memory, ageing, energy, consciousness and identity.

Rejuvenation does not begin with a war against ageing. It begins where our relationship with life begins to change.
10TH INITIATION · 13TH RITE

The Rite of the Womb.

In the contemporary Munay Ki lineage, alongside the first nine rites, there is also the Rite of the Womb, commonly known as the 13th Munay Ki Rite. It is important to make this clear: the 10th initiation and the 13th Munay Ki Rite are names used for the same additional rite — the Rite of the Womb.

Why thirteen rather than ten? The explanation associated with Marcela Lobos emphasizes the symbolism of feminine cycles and the thirteen moons, and the fact that this rite was not conceived simply as a linear continuation of the first nine initiations. It can therefore be received independently of the first nine.

In the contemporary Munay Ki tradition, the Rite of the Womb is connected with a lineage of women from the spiritual traditions of the Amazon who are said to have transmitted the rite to Marcela Lobos, with the intention that she share it with others. Marcela Lobos describes this story in connection with her years of shamanic training and her work with women and medicine people in the Amazon.

The essence of the rite is expressed simply: the womb is not a place where fear and pain must be stored forever; it is a place of creation, life and new beginning.

BLINKITA presents this as an independent feminine remembering and symbolic renewal practice. A woman does not need to have received the first nine Munay Ki rites in order to encounter the Rite of the Womb. Its symbolism is also not limited to the physical organ. It speaks about creativity, relationship with the feminine body, cycles, ancestors, life and what we carry within ourselves.

THE RITE OF THE WOMB

Not a place to store pain. A place from which to create.

The story of the Rite of the Womb is one of the most distinctly feminine layers of contemporary Munay Ki. According to Marcela Lobos' account, she received the transmission from a lineage of women connected with the spiritual traditions of the Amazon and then began sharing it with women around the world. This story belongs to the tradition and its symbolic language; BLINKITA does not present it as a scientifically established historical fact, but as part of the lineage through which the rite is transmitted today.

REJUVENATION · RENEWAL · CONSCIOUSNESS

What does rejuvenation mean in Munay Ki?

Today, the word rejuvenation is almost always trapped inside the image of a younger face, fewer wrinkles, a tighter body and a fight against time. Munay Ki uses a different language. It speaks about renewing our relationship with life, releasing what has become too heavy, and reconnecting with our sense of vitality.

Some Munay Ki teachings also connect this process with a symbolic updating or transformation of the body and with the idea of a movement toward Homo luminous. BLINKITA does not present this as a claim that human cells or DNA literally renew themselves according to a supernatural timetable.

For me, another question matters more: how much of the past does the body continue to carry because we still hold it inside our identity? How much energy goes into repeating the same story? And what happens when we begin to experience life as something we can consciously participate in creating?

Rejuvenation in the BLINKITA language is therefore a process. A process of remembering, releasing, presence, conscious choice and a new relationship with the body and with time.

An initiation is not the end.

You can receive a rite. You can speak the words. You can sit in ceremony, close your eyes and experience something you cannot immediately explain. And then the next morning, life continues.

That is where the real practice begins.

How you speak. How you listen. How you treat your body. How you create boundaries. How you use your power. How you respond to people you do not understand. What you do with your past. What you stop carrying forward. What you begin to create.

Munay Ki can open a door. You still have to walk through it yourself.
